# QuestionEngine Improvement Research — Direct & Concrete Questions

> **Date:** April 2026
> **Purpose:** Research-backed redesign of daily questions to be less vague, more direct, and better at quickly learning about the user.

---

## Problem Statement

Current questions generated by the QuestionEngine feel too open-ended and vague (e.g., "What patterns do you notice in...?"). This creates resistance because:
1. Vague questions require more cognitive effort to answer
2. Open-ended questions feel like an obligation rather than discovery
3. Abstract questions don't yield concrete, actionable information

**Goal:** Replace vague, therapeutic-style questions with direct, specific, and engaging questions that build Nash's understanding quickly while being enjoyable to answer.

---

## Research Findings

### 1. Scaling Questions (SFBT) — The "Workhorses" of Assessment

Scaling questions are described as the "work horses" of solution-focused approaches due to their frequency and versatility.

**Why they work:**
- Make abstract things concrete
- Self-anchored (user defines what numbers mean)
- Quick to answer (reduces resistance)
- Natural follow-up conversation ("Why not lower? What would move you up 1 point?")

**Applications:**
- Priorities, goals, satisfaction, problems
- Coping strategies, motivation for change
- Safety, confidence, treatment progress, hope

**Example patterns:**
```
"Del 1 al 10, ¿qué tan satisfecho estás con [domain]?"
"¿Qué nota le pondrías a tu nivel de energía hoy?"
"En una escala del 1-10, ¿qué tan importante es [value] para ti?"
```

**Follow-up questions:**
- "¿Por qué ese número y no uno más bajo?"
- "¿Qué tendría que pasar para subir un punto?"
- "¿Qué estás haciendo que te mantiene en ese número?"

> **Sources:** [SWEET Institute](https://sweetinstitute.com/solution-focused-brief-therapy-scaling-questions/), [Universal Coach Institute](https://www.universalcoachinstitute.com/scaling-questions/), [PMC Research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10098109/)

### 2. "This or That" Questions — Preference Signals

Binary choice questions reveal preferences quickly and are psychologically rewarding to answer.

**Why they work:**
- Harvard study (2012): Talking about preferences activates the same pleasure centers as food/money/sex
- Low cognitive load (quick 5-second decision)
- The real value is in the follow-up discussion
- Reveals values through accumulated small choices

**Categories:**
- Personality (introvert/extrovert situations)
- Values (career/relationships, money/time, security/freedom)
- Preferences (routine/spontaneity, planning/improvising)
- Lifestyle (urban/rural, early bird/night owl)

**Example patterns:**
```
"¿Prefieres tener más tiempo o más dinero?"
"¿Trabajar solo o en equipo?"
"¿Planificar con detalle o improvisar?"
"¿Decir lo que piensas o mantener la armonía?"
```

**Key insight:** "The question isn't the conversation. The question is just the invitation."

> **Sources:** [Science of People](https://www.scienceofpeople.com/this-or-that-questions/), [Conversation Starters World](https://conversationstartersworld.com/this-or-that-questions/)

---

### 3. Forced-Choice Questions — Reducing Bias

Forced-choice formats are gaining popularity in personality assessment due to their ability to reduce social desirability bias.

**Why they work:**
- Harder to present overly favorable image
- Reduces "neutral" or non-committal answers
- Simpler to answer than free-form questions
- Works well for value prioritization

**Example patterns:**
```
"¿Qué te describe mejor: 'Prefiero liderar' o 'Prefiero seguir'?"
"¿Qué te frustra más: la falta de control o la falta de variedad?"
"Si tuvieras que elegir: ¿seguridad financiera o libertad creativa?"
```

> **Sources:** [ResearchGate - Personality Assessment](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/273684939_Personality_Assessment_Forced-Choice), [Kent Academic Repository](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/44775/1/Personality%20assessment,%20Forced-choice%20FINAL%20for%20sharing.pdf)

### 4. Concrete Experience Questions

Instead of asking about patterns or abstract concepts, ask about specific instances.

**Instead of:** "What patterns do you notice in your relationships?"
**Ask:** "Think of the last time you felt really understood by someone. What happened?"

**Instead of:** "How do you handle stress?"
**Ask:** "Yesterday, what was the most stressful moment? What did you do?"

**Instead of:** "What are your values?"
**Ask:** "When did you last say 'no' to something because it felt wrong?"

**Example patterns:**
```
"¿Cuál fue el mejor momento de tu semana pasada?"
"¿Qué decisión tomaste recientemente de la que estés orgulloso?"
"¿Cuándo fue la última vez que te sentiste realmente en flow?"
"¿Qué hiciste ayer que te dio energía?"
```

---

### 5. Intake Assessment Best Practices

Professional intake assessments balance structure with rapport.

**Key insights:**
- Send administrative paperwork digitally before session
- Alternate between structured questions and exploration
- Make goals specific, time-bound, measurable
- Prioritize: psychiatric diagnosis, psychological processes, relationships

**Effective intake questions:**
```
"¿Qué te gustaría que cambiara en los próximos 3 meses?"
"Si pudieras resolver un solo problema ahora mismo, ¿cuál sería?"
"¿Quién es la persona más importante en tu vida ahora?"
"¿Qué cosa haces bien pero nadie lo reconoce?"
```

> **Sources:** [Quenza](https://quenza.com/blog/intake-form-counseling/), [SimplePractice](https://www.simplepractice.com/resource/therapy-intake-process/), [Simply.Coach](https://simply.coach/blog/essential-therapy-intake-questions/)

## Proposed Question Types (Revised)

### Type 1: Scaling Quick-Check (0-10)
**Purpose:** Rapid domain assessment
**Time to answer:** 5 seconds
**Example:**
```
"Del 1 al 10, ¿cómo te sientes con tu trabajo esta semana?"
Follow-up: "¿Qué tendría que pasar para que sea un punto más?"
```

### Type 2: This-or-That Preference
**Purpose:** Reveal values through choices
**Time to answer:** 5 seconds
**Example:**
```
"¿Más tiempo libre o más dinero?"
"¿Trabajar solo o en equipo?"
```

### Type 3: Last Instance
**Purpose:** Concrete recent experience
**Time to answer:** 30 seconds
**Example:**
```
"¿Cuándo fue la última vez que te sentiste orgulloso de algo?"
"¿Qué fue lo mejor que te pasó esta semana?"
```

### Type 4: Quick Fact
**Purpose:** Fill biographical gaps directly
**Time to answer:** 10 seconds
**Example:**
```
"¿Cuántos años llevas en tu trabajo actual?"
"¿Con quién vives actualmente?"
```

### Type 5: Single Most
**Purpose:** Prioritization and importance
**Time to answer:** 15 seconds
**Example:**
```
"¿Cuál es tu mayor preocupación financiera ahora mismo?"
"¿Qué persona te influenció más en tu infancia?"
```

### Type 6: Tomorrow's Change
**Purpose:** Identify immediate desires
**Time to answer:** 20 seconds
**Example:**
```
"Si mañana pudieras cambiar una cosa de tu rutina, ¿qué sería?"
"¿Qué dejarías de hacer mañana si pudieras?"
```

## Implementation Recommendations

### 1. Mix Question Types Daily
- 2 scaling questions (quick engagement)
- 1 this-or-that (low friction)
- 1 last instance (concrete experience)
- 1 quick fact OR single most (profile building)

### 2. Time Indicators
Show expected answer time: "Esta pregunta toma ~10 segundos"

### 3. Skip Without Guilt
Add explicit "Omitir" button with no negative framing

### 4. Progressive Depth
- Week 1-2: Mostly scaling, this-or-that, quick facts
- Week 3-4: More last instance, single most
- Week 5+: Deeper reflection when trust is built

### 5. Adaptive Selection
- Track richness scores by question type
- Increase frequency of types that yield good responses
- Reduce types that get skipped

### 6. Context Display
- Show why question is being asked: "Para entender mejor tus valores..."
- Show what domain it fills: "Carrera | Relaciones"

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Concrete > Abstract**: Ask about specific instances, not patterns
2. **Quick > Deep**: Low friction questions build consistency
3. **Choice > Open-ended**: Binary choices are easier and still revealing
4. **Follow-up matters**: The real insight comes from "why that number?"
5. **Quantity over quality initially**: Many small data points > few deep ones
6. **Adapt to what works**: Track and optimize based on response richness
